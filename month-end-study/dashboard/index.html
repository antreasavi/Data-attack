Month-end close study

None of the — product-and-window tests clears a strict check for chance once every test is counted. One pattern still repeats across currencies and years. On the last business day of the month, the dollar tends to weaken in the 30 minutes before the London 4pm fix, then win part of it back in the 30 minutes after.

How it was measured

Windows

Three reference times, each with the 30 minutes before and the 30 minutes after: the London 4pm fix (16:00 London), the UK close (16:30 London) and the US close (16:00 New York). The UK close's before-window is the same half hour as the fix's after-window, so those two columns tell one story.

Month-ends

The last business day of each month on the US calendar for the US close and the UK calendar for the other two. Good Friday, Memorial Day and the August bank holiday move the date back a day. The day after Thanksgiving uses the 13:00 New York close, and the last business day of December uses the 12:30 London close.

Prices

  • The last trade at or before each minute, read straight from the Sierra Chart tick files. A window with no trades, or with far fewer trades than the product normally has at that hour, is left out as stale.
  • Futures use the busiest recorded contract in each window. Micro contracts (MES, MNQ, M2K, MGC and others) fill gaps where the full-size contract was not recorded, as MarketWatch does.
  • The currency basket is the equal-weight average of 6E, 6B, 6A, 6J, 6S and 6C on days when at least two were recorded.

Verdicts

  • Strong: still significant after correcting for every test run (q under 0.10), different from normal days (p under 0.05), and the same direction in both halves of the sample. Nothing qualifies.
  • Promising: significant on its own (p under 0.05), different from normal days, and the same direction in both halves.
  • Weak: p under 0.10 on the average or on the up/down count.
  • Too few months: fewer than 12 month-ends recorded.

Trade plans

  • Each Promising test is replayed from the minute-by-minute prices: every entry and exit on a grid across the hour around the reference time, in the test's direction, with a range of stops from the entry or none.
  • A stop is hit when a minute's low (for a long) or high (for a short) reaches it, and it fills exactly at the stop. A fast market around the fix may fill worse.
  • The plan in the table takes the entry and exit with the best average result with no stop, then the stop that made the most per tick risked, averaged with its neighbouring stops so one lucky level can't win. If no stop makes money, it keeps no stop.
  • Out of sample: each month-end is scored with the plan chosen from all the other month-ends, so the plan never sees the month it is scored on.
  • Second check: the month-end average beats 95% of random sets of normal days of the same size, its bootstrap 95% range leaves out zero, the median month-end moves the same way, it holds without its largest month, and at least half of the years agree. Confirmed means all five pass.
  • The currency basket is not one contract, so its plans are in bp with each member's costs averaged.

Limits

  • Most products have only one year of recordings, which is too few month-ends to separate a habit from luck. 6B goes back to 2012, 6E to late 2022, ZF and ZN to 2023–24.
  • About 1 in 20 tests will look significant by chance, which is why the corrected q matters more than any single p.
  • The tests are before costs: Avg ticks and Avg per contract show the gross move. The trade plans are after costs.
  • This is a historical study of recorded prices, not a recommendation to trade.
